# Pricing

Charge something. From the start. More than you think.

## When to use

Setting the price for your product, or reevaluating an existing price.

## The principle

Free is not a business model. Charge from day one. Price based on the value you deliver, not the cost to you. Most founders undercharge — your first instinct on price should be doubled.

## How to apply

1. **Start with the value, not the cost.** How much time/money/pain does your product save? Price at 10-20% of that value.

2. **Use simple pricing.** One plan. Maybe two. Not a pricing page with a comparison table and an "enterprise" tier with "contact us." You're serving 100 people, not 10,000.

3. **Charge more than feels comfortable.** If nobody pushes back on your price, it's too low. You want ~20% of people to say "that's expensive" — it means the other 80% see the value.

4. **Annual > monthly.** Offer both, make annual cheaper. Annual customers churn less and give you runway.

5. **Raise prices for new customers.** Grandfather existing customers at their original price. Raise for every new cohort as the product gets better.

## Output

- Your price and the reasoning behind it
- The value calculation: what does the customer save/gain?
- Comparison to alternatives (including "do nothing")
- A plan for when and how to raise prices

## Anti-patterns

- Free tiers that cannibalize paid conversion
- Complex pricing that requires a sales call to understand
- Pricing based on what competitors charge instead of value delivered
- "We'll figure out monetization later"
