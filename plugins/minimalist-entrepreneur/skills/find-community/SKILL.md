# Find Community

Find your people before you build your product. The community comes first.

## When to use

At the very start — before you have an idea, a product, or a company. Or when you have a product but no users and need to figure out who actually cares.

## The principle

Minimalist entrepreneurs don't start with a product idea. They start with a community they belong to and understand. You build for people you know, not strangers you imagine.

## How to apply

1. **List communities you already belong to.** Subreddits, Discord servers, Slack workspaces, forums, local meetups, professional associations, open source projects. Where do you already spend time?

2. **For each community, identify pain points.** What do people complain about? What takes too long? What's broken? What do they wish existed?

3. **Rank by these criteria:**
   - Do you genuinely care about this community? (Not "is it big" — do you care?)
   - Can you reach 100 people in it directly? (DMs, posts, in person)
   - Are they already spending money on solutions? (Even bad ones)
   - Do you have credibility here? (They know you, trust your judgment)

4. **Pick one.** Go deep, not wide.

## Output

A document with:
- The community you're choosing and why
- 3-5 specific pain points you've identified
- How you'll reach your first 100 people
- Your existing credibility/connection to this community

## Anti-patterns

- Picking a community because "the market is huge" when you don't belong to it
- Trying to serve multiple communities at once
- Skipping this step because you already have a product idea you're excited about
